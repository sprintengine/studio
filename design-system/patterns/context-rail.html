Content starts three columns in. Each one is a list of things to choose, so nothing says which choice the screen is actually about.

Three navigation columns before the first word of content. A surface that seems to need two levels of rail folds the outer level into a group of the one rail, as above — never into a column of its own.

Anatomy

  • Brand row. Fixed above the rail. Outside the swap.
  • Create + narrow. The surface's New … row and its search/filter lead the rail and stay pinned while the list scrolls — the same anatomy on every surface, so there is one place to look.
  • Groups. One list, or several under quiet headings. A heading earns its place only by separating one group from another: over a lone list, or over a list whose search field already names it, it is the same word said twice.
  • Back. A chevron leading the surface's bar, immediately before the surface's name. It restores the rail the surface replaced, and Escape does the same. Still one affordance and one answer — the rail carries none.
    It was a rail row pinned to the column's bottom, and that is the one thing this pattern got wrong. Below a scrollport is the last place the eye goes and the furthest point from the title of the thing being left, so the most-used control on the surface sat where it cost the most to reach. Attaching it to the name reads as "leave this" rather than "go back", which also keeps it distinct from the window's own history back/forward pair elsewhere in the chrome.

The alignment grid

A rail does not get a column to itself — it replaces one, and several rails take turns in the same physical column. So the grid below is not this pattern's house style; it is the contract that lets one rail follow another, and follow the app sidebar it displaces, without the text edge moving. Ruled 2026-08-05 after all three candidate grids were measured and none of them was being met.

A door rail is a composition: this pattern's column (pinned head, Back row, the swap) carrying list-surface's two-line row. That is why neither pattern alone described the shipped rails, and why the reconciled numbers live here rather than in either one.

  • Title x-offset: 36px. The number everything else serves. It is 4 + 8 + 16 + 8 — scrollport inset, row padding, leading icon slot, gap — and it is where the app sidebar's own workspace rows put their text, so drilling in does not re-flow the column.
  • Scrollport inset space.2xs (4px), matching list-surface's .rail-rows. Not 6px, and not the 10px that shipped: no pattern has ever specified 10px anywhere.
  • Head inset space.sm (8px), matching list-surface's .rail-head. Wider than the scrollport on purpose — full-width controls against inset row fills.
  • Row padding space.2xs / space.sm, gap space.sm — .cr-row, unchanged.
  • Leading icon slot icon.size.sm (16px), fixed and reserved by the rail, never sized by the surface filling it. A surface may render a smaller mark inside it; it may not change the box. This is the whole of the glyph-slot ruling and the only reason 36px is stable.
  • Row type: title font.size.body (13px), state line font.size.meta (12px) — list-surface's .rail-row. The shipped rails ran one step smaller on both.
  • Group heading inset 8px, aligned to the row's fill inner edge rather than to its title.
  • Back chevron: a size.control.xs box on the bar's leading edge — .cr-back, icon-only, no label. In a bar that is indented to align its title with the canvas below it, the chevron takes that line and the title follows: the box hangs back by half a step so the glyph, not the button's edge, sits on the grid. Align the box instead and the indent reads as 8px too deep.
  • The empty-filter notice belongs to the rail, rendered inside the scrollport under the head at the row inset. As a sibling of a flex: 1 rail it is pushed to the bottom of the column when the list is empty — furthest from the search field that emptied it.
  • The rich row (door-rails-premium, 2026-09-08). A rail whose rows carry a context line (where the thing lives, with a clock at the trailing edge), a title, and a line of marks and chips under it is wearing the app sidebar's row, not this pattern's two-line row — the Automations door does, so a run reads like the chat beside it. Its title lands on the sidebar's flat-stream edge (16px: 4px scrollport + 12px row padding) at font.size.heading, with no leading icon slot; the lifecycle mark moves down onto the detail line. It also hovers on --bg-surface-raised rather than --bg-hover and wears the sidebar's inset selection ring — the sidebar row's own fills. Those three are the one sanctioned departure from the column above, and they exist so that drilling from the sidebar into the Automations door re-flows nothing: the row you left and the row you arrive at are the same row.

Measured, not assumed: the app sidebar's own comment claimed a 36px text column for all four of its row families, but its top-nav rows compute to 40px and its section headers to 38px — the comment was written against a 12px icon that shipped at 16px. 36px is what its workspace rows and fold rows actually use, and those are the rows a door rail replaces.

Selection in a two-level rail

  • The rail keeps one focused selection, at bg.selected: the thing the canvas is showing.
  • A group carrying outer context — which project, which automation — rests at bg.selected-resting permanently, whether or not the rail holds focus. It answers "where am I?", and it must not compete with "what am I looking at?".
  • Mark the group, never infer it from position: which group is the outer one belongs to the surface.

Motion

  • The incoming rail slides in from its leading edge over motion.duration.normal on motion.ease.standard — opacity and one transform, nothing that reflows.
  • Replacement in the same column at the same width is precisely the change a hard cut reads as a glitch: without the slide, everything in the column changes at once with nothing to say it arrived.
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the treatment drops out entirely. A shortened slide is a stutter.
